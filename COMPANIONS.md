# Legend Quest: the companions

This is the companion bible: who the people who walk beside you are, what they want, what they
need, how they talk, what they approve of, and where each of them is going across Acts 1 to 3.
`STORY.md` (Caleb's script) is the source of truth for every fixed beat; this file deepens it and
never contradicts it. Where it invents (a name, a village, a memory), it invents round what the
script already says, so a line in the script reads truer for knowing this.

It's written for whoever writes the next scene. Read a companion's entry before you write a word
of theirs. If a line you've written could be said by any of them, it isn't theirs yet.

## Research: the principles

What I read before writing anyone, and what I took from it. Principles only: nothing here is
copied from another game's characters or lines.

**1. Want, need, wound, lie, truth.** A character arc is the distance between what a character
believes and what's true. The *wound* (Truby's "ghost") is the backstory event; the *lie* is the
reasonable-sounding conclusion they drew from it; the *want* is the lie in action, the thing they
chase because of it; the *need* is the truth they'd have to accept to be whole. The wound makes
them cling to the lie because the lie seems to protect them (K. M. Weiland). So every companion
here gets one concrete memory, not a summary, and their lie is something a sensible person could
believe after living through it. In Legend Quest every lie is a different answer to the game's one
question: *what do you do when nobody with power comes?*

| | The Soul-Bound | Sera | Mira | Kael | Brannock |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Their answer | Become the power | Need nobody | Trust only what's written | Take what you're owed | Stop promising |
| The truth | Power without consent is Harrow with a better excuse | A forest is a neighbourhood, not a wall | You can love someone and still say no to them | The people who stay when you're no use are the ones who count | Loyalty is owed sideways, to whoever's holding the line beside you |

**2. Approval is a worldview, not a score.** The companion systems that work make each companion
read the same event through a different worldview, so one choice is cheered by one and frowned at
by another for reasons the player can see coming. I want every affinity number in the script to
be predictable from the tables below. If a writer has to guess whether Sera approves, the table is
wrong. Companions approve of *why* you did something, not only what happened: Brannock approves of
holding a line, whether or not it held.

**3. Reactivity is acknowledgement.** The cheapest, strongest reactivity is a line that shows a
companion noticed what you did three scenes ago. Every companion conversation here checks earlier
flags (the crystal, the crowd, the court) and says something different.

**4. A personal quest is the argument against the lie.** Each companion's quest puts their lie
under load. The good outcome is the companion acting on the truth; the bad outcome is the lie
winning, and it costs something later that the player can feel (Brannock's survival on the stairs,
which side Kael stands on, whether Sera says yes to the Soul-Bound's offer).

**5. Romance: trust before attraction, actions before declarations.** Slow burn works when the
audience sees the pull before the characters admit it, when trust is built by things done rather
than said, and when the beats escalate: noticing, a small vulnerability, a first deliberate touch,
a near-miss, and only then a declaration. Each romance here is tied to the companion's need, so
falling for you *is* their arc, not a side room off it. Romance never skips the need.

**6. Banter is short, local and survivable.** Barks and banter have to be brief, specific to the
place, and survive being cut. In this engine a banter line whose speaker isn't in the party is
skipped, so every companion line in a banter is written to stand on its own: the Soul-Bound and
you carry the spine, and companions add asides. Characters reveal themselves by what they notice:
the Soul-Bound sees who owns things, Sera sees animals, Mira sees dates, Kael sees exits and
prices, Brannock sees chokepoints.

**7. The friend who becomes the antagonist.** The sympathetic antagonist has a sincere belief in
their own good, a worldview as coherent as the hero's, a grounded motive from their past, and an
argument aimed straight at a value the hero holds. A former friend makes the stakes personal. The
script's models (a dream that justifies any cost; righteous anger at a system that fails people)
are used here as principles only. The Soul-Bound's slide is built from small, reasonable steps,
each one an answer to a real failure, so the audience agrees with them about half the time.

**8. Betrayal blindness.** People tend not to see betrayal by someone they depend on, to protect
the relationship (Jennifer Freyd). That's the player's position, and I use it on purpose: the
comforting answer to the Soul-Bound is always on offer and always raises Bond, and the honest
answer usually raises Bond too. The player is never punished for loving them; they're only ever
given the chance to look.

**9. Grounded wounds.** I researched each companion's wound so it behaves like the real thing:

- **Brannock: moral injury.** Jonathan Shay defines it as a betrayal of what's right, by someone
  holding legitimate authority, in a high-stakes situation; Litz and colleagues widen it to acts
  a person did or failed to prevent that break their own moral code. Its marks are guilt, shame,
  self-condemnation and withdrawal, and Shay notes that the person's ideals and attachments shrink.
  Brannock was betrayed by the crown *and* believes he betrayed his men by promising them relief in
  the crown's name. He drinks to stop counting.
- **Mira: ambiguous loss and refugee grief.** Pauline Boss's ambiguous loss is a loss that isn't
  total, someone physically gone but psychologically present, or the reverse, and it freezes
  grief. Research on refugees describes exactly this when family are missing in the country left
  behind, and the loss of home itself as a kind of it. Mira doesn't know if her father is dead or
  one of the Hollowed, so she can't mourn him, and she loved a teacher who is still alive and is
  a monster.
- **Kael: scarcity.** Mullainathan and Shafir describe poverty as a "bandwidth tax": scarcity
  narrows attention to the next shortfall and leaves less room to plan or trust. (Some of the
  original studies have been questioned in replication; I use the lived pattern, not the numbers.)
  Kael is always pricing things, always counting exits, always one rent ahead of the next disaster.
- **Sera: solastalgia.** Glenn Albrecht's word for the distress of watching your home environment
  damaged while you still live in it: homesickness without leaving home. Sera's forest is rotting
  round her, and it's the second time.

## How the numbers work

**Approval** is `affinity-<id>` in `Story/Meters.cs`, 0 to 100, starting at 20 (Valencia at 10).
Every change in a script uses one of these sizes, so the tables below predict it:

| Size | Amount | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A nod | +2 / -2 | A small thing they like or dislike, said in passing |
| Real | +3 / -3 | Something that touches what they care about |
| Big | +5 / -5 | You acted on their values (or against them) when it cost you |
| Defining | +10 / -10 | A turning point of their arc or their personal quest |

Romance follows the script: a romance needs approval 60 and that companion's flirt flags. The
flirt ladder is `<id>-flirt-1` (from the scene where you meet), then `<id>-flirt-2` (from a
companion conversation, which only offers it once approval is 25 or more), then the script's own
romance beats. Nobody is ever pushed into a romance: every flirt option is marked with a heart and
is only one of three or four.

**Bond** (closeness to the Soul-Bound, starts 50) and **Ambition** (their drift towards power,
starts 10) are not approval. Bond is closeness, not agreement:

- Bond rises when you're *with* them: when you stand beside them in public, tell them the truth in
  private, stop them gently ("Enough. He's done." is +3 in the script), remember things together.
- Bond falls when you side with the powerful against the small, apologise for them to their face,
  lie to them, or leave them standing alone.
- Ambition rises when you endorse "whatever it takes", let them act unchecked, feed the dream of
  rule, or when the world rewards them for going fast. It almost never falls; the slide is a ratchet.

So the honest friend and the loyal friend both keep Bond high. Only the flatterer keeps Ambition
climbing too. That's the design: the player who loves the Soul-Bound *and* looks at them clearly
gets the High Bond ending, and still can't stop the betrayal, because the betrayal is fixed.

## The Soul-Bound

> *"So here's the plan. We go to Fable. We make a name. Then a bigger name."*

**Pitch.** The best person you know, who learned at twelve that being good without being powerful
just gets the people you love punished, and has been getting bigger ever since.

### Background

You grew up together in **Kettle Cove**, a fishing town of forty-odd households on **Marrow
Isle**, the outermost of the southern isles. The isles' fishing rights belong to **Lord Harrow of
Heronsgate**, whose badge is a grey heron on green. Harrow never lived on Marrow; he sent his
reeve, **Doggett**, twice a year.

- **Eight.** A storm, a capsized boat, you in the water. {sb} dove in off the pier when the adults
  were still shouting about ropes. (P-03.) {sb}'s mother, **Corra**, a net-mender, wrapped you
  both in the same blanket and said, "Well. Now you've got each other." You did.
- **Twelve: the nets.** Harrow raised the tithe in a bad season, and when the cove couldn't pay,
  Doggett's men took the nets. Every net in the cove, off the drying racks, into a cart. Corra
  went down on her knees in the mud of the landing and begged, and Lord Harrow, who had come over
  himself that once to see the cart filled, talked to his reeve about the weather and did not look
  at her. (P-04.) That winter the cove ate limpets and boiled kelp.
- **Twelve: the skiff.** This is {sb}'s defining memory, and yours. One night in the hungry winter,
  the two of you took old Bram's skiff out with hand-lines into Harrow's reserved water, the deep
  channel nobody was allowed to fish, and came back before dawn with a sack of herring. {sb}
  carried it door to door and for one morning the whole cove ate. At noon Doggett stood in the
  square and asked who had fished the lord's water. {sb} stood up, alone, and didn't look at you.
  The fine was Corra's wedding ring. She paid it, and never said one word to {sb} about it. That
  silence is the wound. The lesson {sb} took from it: *you can only do right safely once you're
  big enough that nobody can make the people you love pay for it.*
- **Sixteen: the red moon.** The oath. *Never leave the other behind.* (P-03.)
- **Sixteen to twenty-four.** Sellswords along the southern coast: caravan guards, a pirate crew
  run off a lighthouse, a lost dowry found in a well. Never enough to buy back anything. Then
  rumours of Fable, a rich kingdom that pays in gold and thanks, and two passages on the *Gull's
  Wager*.

{sb}'s race, class, look and pronouns are the player's. Nothing above depends on them. Write
round them: never "{sb}'s pointed ears", never a verb that has to agree with `{sb_they}`
("{sb_they} is" breaks for "they"); use the name, or a modal ("{sb_they}'ll", "{sb_they} can").

### Want, need, wound, lie, truth

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Want** | A kingdom of their own, where nobody kneels in the mud. Under it: never to be small again. |
| **Need** | To accept that nobody can carry everyone, and that the slow, shared, consenting way (a council, a petition, a friend saying no) is not weakness. To let you hold them back. |
| **Wound** | Corra on her knees before a lord who didn't look at her; the ring paid for {sb}'s herring. |
| **Lie** | Goodness without power just gets the people you love punished. Be big first; then be good. |
| **Truth** | The cove fed itself that morning because people shared, not because anyone ruled. Power taken without consent is Harrow with a better excuse. |

### Voice

Warm, quick, physical. Jokes as a reflex under pressure, especially when the subject is real.
Short sentences that tumble into a long one when excited. Says "we" far more than "I". Teases you
constantly and means none of it. Remembers everything about home: names, smells, who owed whom.

The pronoun drift is the arc in miniature, and every writer should follow it:

- **Act 1:** "we". *"We make a name."* *"Let's go be the reason somebody gets to come home."*
- **Act 2:** "I" creeps in. *"I'm going to fix this. I'm going to fix all of it."*
- **Act 3:** "you", meaning other people's understanding. *"You'll see I was right."* And "them",
  meaning everyone, said tenderly. *"I'm doing this for them."*

**Never says:** "peasant", "rabble", "obey" (not even in Act 3; {sb} says "keep safe"), "my
people" before the end of Act 2, anything cruel *for pleasure*. {sb}'s cruelty is always
efficiency and always explained.

**Sample lines (Act 1):**
- "Triple? Sold." (the script's)
- "Somebody in there is doing the Watch's job for them."
- "Imagine owning this. Your own field, your own mill, nobody's reeve at the gate."
- "Tell me when it does. Every time I sound like him. Promise?"

### Bond and Ambition, by what you do

| Raises Bond | Lowers Bond | Raises Ambition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing beside {sb} in public (backing them at court: +5) | Siding with the powerful against the small (the sergeant: -5) | "Whatever it takes", "Then we get big" (+3) |
| Stopping them gently, as a friend (+3 to +5) | Apologising for them to their face (-2) | Letting them act unchecked; saying nothing (+3) |
| Telling the truth in private, even a hard one (+2 to +3) | Lying to them (-2) | Feeding the dream of rule, calling them "Sir {sb}" (+2) |
| Shared memories, the oath, home (+2 to +5) | Leaving them alone with it ("Let it go", -2) | The world rewarding them for going fast (fixed beats) |

### You, the companions, and the slide

{sb} loves you the way you love a limb: without thinking about it, and with real terror at the
idea of losing it. You're the one person who's allowed to say "enough". That's why A3-C2-04 breaks
the bond rather than killing you: {sb} cuts the oath *to save you* from what they're about to do.

How {sb} sees the others in Act 1: Sera "is wrong, but I like her" and watches {sb} "like a wolf
that hasn't decided yet", which stings; Mira is someone {sb} desperately wants to be forgiven by
after Thornbridge; Kael is a younger version of {sb} with worse luck, and {sb} recruits him on
sight, the first time {sb} gives someone a place; Brannock is the father figure who sees through
{sb} at once ("That's how every warlord I ever fought started talking"), and {sb} wants his
respect more than anyone's but yours.

**The slide, step by step.** Every step must read as reasonable in the moment. Here is the inner
logic, so a writer can keep {sb} sympathetic at each one:

| Beat | What happens | What {sb} learns (the lie, confirmed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1-C1 Docks | Blade at Grell's throat | Bullies only stop when someone bigger stops them. |
| A1-C2 Quarry | Wants the crystal smashed now | Slow costs lives. (True, this once.) |
| A1-C3 Thornbridge | The Watch watched; the crowd chants {sb}'s name | People follow whoever comes. Nobody else came. |
| A1-C4 Court | The king thanks, then weighs; Crane says "not yet" | Good men are slow. Someone has to be fast. |
| A1-C6 Greywatch | Picks up the shard; the Voice speaks in {sb}'s own voice | The Voice never says anything {sb} hasn't already thought. It *agrees*. |
| A1-C6-05 | Keeps the shard "just to understand it" | The first secret, framed as responsibility. |
| A1-C7-03 | Turns Crane instead of exposing him | The second secret, framed as strategy. And it works. |
| A1-C8 | Nearly touches the Box; "I'll never fall like he did" | Sincere. That's the tragedy. |
| A2-C1-03 | Learns the crown was built on the Purging Dawn | Every crown is built on someone's bones. Even the good king's. |
| A2-C3-02 | Hollowmere's rulers "who actually deliver" | Order without consent can still feed people. |
| A2-C4-04 | Burns Severin, and Valencia, with the Eye | Power held for one second ends the threat. Then Crane takes it. |
| A2-C5-03 | Kills (or nearly kills) Crane | A man who sold everyone is ended by the only person willing to. |
| A2-C6-06 | Leon names {sb} Lord Protector | The system itself hands {sb} the key. |
| A3-C1-01 | The vision of the ending | Something is coming that no council can vote away. |
| A3-C2-03 | The Box snaps; Leon dies | {sb} didn't mean it. After the horror, the calm: no one left to hesitate. |
| A3-C2-04 | Breaks the Soul-Bond | "I can't carry you *and* save them." Love, as a reason to cut. |
| A3-C3-05 | Freezes a district "to keep them safe" | {sb} becomes the vision and doesn't recognise their own face. |

**The rules of the slide (heartbreaking, never cartoonish):**

1. {sb} is right about the problem every single time. They're wrong about the answer.
2. {sb} never sneers at the weak. Every escalation is to protect someone small.
3. {sb} apologises sincerely, and then does it again. The apology is real; so is the second time.
4. {sb} always offers you a way out, and always offers you a place beside them.
5. {sb} still jokes in Act 3, and still remembers your birthday. On your birthday in Act 3, a
   parcel reaches the resistance camp in the Mirewood with no note: a little herring carved from
   driftwood, the way {sb} used to whittle them in the hungry winter. Nobody else knows what it means.
6. Their last act, at every Bond tier, is aimed at the real enemy (A3-C5-04).

### Personal quest: The Heron's Due

- **Hook (Act 1, Ch 4, `sb-talk-3`):** in the gallery at court, clapping for you, a man in a green
  coat with a heron on the breast. Lord Harrow, in Questeria, at the king's own court. He looked
  straight at {sb} and didn't know them. Sets `sb-quest-hook`.
- **Middle (Act 1, Ch 7, the winter):** with the banners called, Harrow petitions the crown to
  raise the southern isles' tithe to a third of every catch "for the war". War as a pretext; the
  isles will starve for it. {sb} wants him stopped. Two roads:
  - *The slow way:* you and {sb} bring the isles' case to Princess Elara with the fishers of
    Saltwind (Old Tomas speaks for them) and letters from Kettle Cove. It takes the whole winter.
  - *The fast way:* {sb} uses Crane (after A1-C7-03, `sb-controls-crane`) to dig up Harrow's debts
    and ruins him in a week, quietly.
- **Resolution, good (`sb-heron-petition`):** Harrow's petition is refused and the crown reviews
  the old tithe; the cove gets its nets back by royal writ. Corra writes. Harrow, in the hall,
  finally asks {sb} who they are. {sb}: "Nobody. I was on the landing." Bond +5, Ambition -3.
  *And the tragedy:* in Act 3's dream-visit {sb} can say, "The petition worked. I know. It took
  nine months. I counted the winter it cost." Even the good outcome feeds the lie. But it's also
  the one argument {sb} can't answer, and you can use it there.
- **Resolution, bad (`sb-heron-ruined`):** Harrow is ruined and the tithe falls with him, fast and
  clean. Crane now knows something about {sb}. Ambition +5. {sb} enjoyed it, and is frightened
  of how much.

### At the betrayal

{sb} doesn't want you dead and never will. At A3-C2-04 they send the guards away so nobody sees
them beg. Whatever you say, they cut the bond with the Crown's light because they believe you'll
be safer far from them, and because they can't do what's coming with you watching. The Bond-tier
line is the measure of how much of them is still yours.

## Sera Vantis

> *"So we find it and understand it. Then we decide."*

**Pitch.** A warden who has kept watch over one stretch of forest for forty-one years, mostly
alone, because the last time she trusted kind strangers, they cut her mother's forest down.

### Background

Sera is Sylvan, about a hundred and forty, young for her people and old for a ranger. Her mother,
**Ithiel Vantis**, was warden of the Mirewood's western reach before her, and the reach included
**the Hollins**, a stand of four hundred silverbarks older than Questeria.

- **The defining memory: the honey cakes.** Forty-one years ago, in the last war, the crown needed
  warships. Surveyors came to the Mirewood: polite men, soft-spoken, who brought Ithiel honey
  cakes from the city and asked Sera, then barely grown by Sylvan count, to show them the old
  paths. She did, proudly. A month later the Hollins was stumps and sawdust, marked and felled
  from her own directions. One silverbark was left because it was too young to be worth the axe.
  Ithiel went to Questeria to protest, was held for "obstructing the crown's timber", came home
  grey, gave Sera the reach that winter, and never went to the city again. She faded twelve years
  later, still not speaking of it.
- The Rangers' Lodge (Ranger Tamsin's lodge) counts Sera as one of its wardens and mostly forgets
  she exists. She likes it that way.
- She has a grey-winged kestrel called **Burr**, who is usually off scouting. (Mention him in
  passing until combat gives him a sprite.)
- Every warden keeps a **count**: old trees, dens, springs, setts. Sera's is in her head. Three
  weeks before you arrive, the count started going wrong.

### Want, need, wound, lie, truth

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Want** | To keep the Mirewood whole, by herself, without needing anyone. |
| **Need** | To let people in. The forest's health depends on the woodcutters, the Lodge, the city; you can't guard a forest from the people who live beside it. |
| **Wound** | The surveyors' kindness, and her own directions marked on the trees. Then the rot: her home damaged round her a second time (solastalgia). |
| **Lie** | People are kind when they want something. Animals only want what they show you. |
| **Truth** | A forest is a neighbourhood, not a wall. Some people stay kind when they've got nothing to gain. |

### Voice

Dry, few words, flat statements. Observes before she speaks and speaks only what she's observed.
Uses nature literally, never poetically: she doesn't say the forest "weeps", she says "three otters
dead below the falls". Counts things. Deadpan humour that lands a beat late. Deflects compliments.
When she's moved she goes shorter, not longer.

**Never says:** "I feel", "friend" (until she means it), "please" (unless it's serious), "magical",
"destiny". Never swears by gods; swears by weather ("Rot and rain.").

**Sample lines:**
- "You're loud. Both of you." (the script's)
- "Don't touch it. The rot goes into skin as easily as wood."
- "I like people fine. At a distance. With the wind blowing towards me."
- "I don't want to be right about this. I've been right about people before. It never once made me happy."

### Approval

| Sera approves | | Sera disapproves | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding before acting; patience | +3 | Smashing first, asking after | -3 to -5 |
| Protecting animals and wild places | +3 to +5 | Waste; taking more than you need | -3 |
| Owning a mistake and asking how to fix it | +3 | Boasting; flattery from strangers | -2 |

### Voice

"monster" about herself (she says "a thing like me"); never "hunger"; calls the sun "the old
enemy" with affection.

**Sample lines:** "You're staring. Most visitors stare at the stained glass." (the script's)

### Approval

Approves: protecting her from the Order; asking rather than assuming; mercy to thralls and Tithe
villagers; honesty about {sb}; letting her choose. Disapproves: treating her as the key or the
weapon (-5); the Order's "usual way"; calling the Tithe "fair"; burning anything with the Eye
(-10 if you let {sb}'s beam run).

### You, {sb}, and the others

She smells the Black Box on {sb} the moment they shake hands (`valencia-sensed-shard`). {sb}

keep, that silence is her guilt. {sb}'s beam at Sunfall burns her; whether she forgives it is the
`affinity-valencia-sb` meter. She gets on with Mira (two women who loved the wrong teacher), is
wary of Brannock until he holds Corwin back for her, and enjoys Kael enormously.

The coup is at dawn. Valencia can stand in daylight now (the sun came back), but she watches from

She knew. She tells you so before anyone asks.

## Princess Elara Aurelian

> *"Because I don't know which of them to trust."*

**Pitch.** The heir who wants to fix the crown from the inside, impatient with every slow thing
about it, and so the first person in Fable to understand exactly how dangerous {sb} is.

### Background

Elara is twenty-four, King Leon's only child. Her mother, **Queen Amarante**, died of a summer
fever when Elara was ten, while the court spent two days arguing over which physician had the
right of precedence to treat a queen. Elara has distrusted procedure ever since, and has spent
fourteen years learning to use it anyway. She rides out alone (the townsfolk gossip about it)
because it's the only time nobody is deciding anything for her.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Want** | To be taken seriously: her father's equal, not his decoration. |
| **Need** | To trust people she can't control, and to rule by listening. |
| **Wound** | Her mother, dying while the court argued precedence. |
| **Lie** | I have to do everything myself, because nobody else will move. (The same lie as {sb}'s. Hers loses.) |
| **Truth** | A crown is a promise to listen. Her father's: "I'd rather be told the truth rudely than lies politely." |

**Voice:** quick, commanding, impatient, funny when she forgets herself. Never "please" to a
courtier; always "thank you" to a guard (her father's habit).

**Approval:** approves of results, discretion, and people who tell her the truth; disapproves of
flattery, bowing in public, and anyone who calls her father weak.

**With {sb}:** at the knighting she watches the crowd chant {sb}'s name and says, "That's a
powerful thing, in a war." She's the first to measure {sb} politically. In Act 3 she's the one
whose father {sb} killed, and she leads the resistance anyway, beside the person who loved {sb}
most. Not a romance: the two of you are equals in a war.

**At the betrayal:** she flees into the Low Ring sewers. Sera (or Kael) gets her out.
