# Brief: fix art splitting, bad cropping and animation problems in LegendQuest

This brief is for an AI assistant (ChatGPT or Codex) working on the LegendQuest repository. It explains how the
character art is built and animated, lists the problems that have been found (with numbers and file locations), and
sets out an ordered plan with acceptance checks. Read all of it before changing anything.

- Repository on this machine: `C:\Users\Owner\OneDrive\Documents\LegendQuest` (also on GitHub at
  `https://github.com/calebkkiatoukaysi/LegendQuest`). Run every command from the repository root.
- Branch to start from: `feature/realtime-combat` (commit `43a6892` or later). Make your own branch from it, for
  example `fix/art-splitting`.
- **Evidence for every problem below is in `docs/art-audit/`**: images in `evidence/`, the audit's measurements in
  `border.json` and `wcheck.json`, and the scripts that produced them in `scripts/` (§2.0). Open the images before
  you start.
- The game: a MonoGame/C# (.NET 9) 2D fantasy RPG. Characters are jointed "paper-doll" rigs built from painted parts;
  some monsters are drawn from sprite sheets. The art pipeline is Python 3.12 (numpy, Pillow, scipy).

## 0. Rules you must follow

1. **Original content only.** The game takes inspiration from how AdventureQuest Worlds plays, never from what it
   owns. Do not use, trace, copy or download any AdventureQuest/AQW (or any other game's) art, names, characters or
   sound. Any art you paint must be LegendQuest's own, in the style described in `Story/03-art-overhaul.md` §2 and
   `Story/13-art-prompts.md` §2.
2. **`Content/Characters/Parts/clips.json` must keep LF line endings.** It is written only by
   `python tools/rig/clips.py`; never edit it by hand. Check with `python tools/rig/clips.py --check`.
3. **`tools/import_parts.py` is slow (25 to 70 minutes) and rewrites the atlases, `parts.json`, part of
   `Content/Content.mgcb` and part of `ASSETS.md`.** While it runs, do not edit `Story/` or `ASSETS.md`. Run it once,
   after all source-art and pipeline changes for a batch are done, not after every small change.
4. **Run `python tools/check_assets.py` before every commit.** Every file under `Content/` and `tools/source/`, and
   every script in `tools/`, must be listed in `ASSETS.md` (with author, tool and licence). Every `Content/` file must
   be in `Content/Content.mgcb`. The generated sections of `ASSETS.md` (between `BEGIN`/`END` comments) are written by
   the tools' `assets` modes (`python tools/part_sheet.py assets`, `python tools/weapon_alone.py assets`,
   `python tools/dress_pose.py assets`) and by `import_parts.py`. Do not hand-edit those sections.
5. **Keep animation timing.** The combat engine (`Combat/Realtime/MotionTimings.cs`) reads each clip's `hit`,
   `release` and `recover` event times and its length from `clips.json`. Damage lands at those times, so moving them
   changes game balance. When you change a clip's poses, keep those times unless there's a stated reason. If any of
   them change, run `--simulate all 200` and `--simulate classes 100` before and after and report the difference.
6. **Update the docs when the design changes** (`README.md`, `Story/03-art-overhaul.md`, `Story/13-art-prompts.md`,
   `Story/11-combat.md` §8). Code comments and docs are plain, precise British English; match the file you edit.
7. **Git:** commit under the repository owner's git identity (already set in the repo's local config) with **no
   co-author or "generated by" lines**. Work on your own branch. Do not push to `master`, open a pull request or
   rewrite history unless the owner asks.
8. **Report honestly:** say what you verified and how, what you couldn't verify, and what is still rough.

## 1. How the character art works

### 1.1 Source art → parts (Python, offline)

All source art is in the repository under `tools/source/characters/`:

| Folder | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `base-male.png`, `base-female.png` | The mannequins (1024×1536, on black), plus `*-blank.png` heads. |
| `bodies/<base>/<bone>.png` | Bodies painted in pieces, one PNG per bone (14 bones). |
| `armors/<id>/<bone>.png` | Armors in pieces, cut from `whole/<id>.png` by `tools/dress_pose.py`. |
| `faces/`, `hairs/`, `helms/`, `capes/`, `extras/`, `sheets/` | Head pieces and painted part sheets. |
| `weapon-sheets/weapons-NN.png` + `.json` | Painted weapon sheets (4 columns). The `.json` lists each column's id and hand, e.g. `weapon-brannock/near/blade`, `weapon-brannock/far/shield`. |
| `weapons-alone/<id>[.far].png` + `.json` | Each item cut out alone, upright, with its grip, kind and sheet. `.far` is the far-hand half of a two-handed item. |
| `weapons/<id>.png` | The base figure holding the item (made by `tools/weapon_alone.py`). |

The tools, in pipeline order:

- **`tools/rig/skeleton.py`**: bones (`BONES`), joints (`JOINTS`), tips, draw order (`DRAW`), and the joint "balls"
  (`BALL`) that keep a bent joint from opening a gap.
- **`tools/rig/scissors.py`** and **`tools/body_parts.py`**: cut a body into bone pieces along painted lines. Each piece
  carries on under the piece in front of it, so it can turn without opening a gap.
- **`tools/dress_pose.py`** (`template | cut | preview | assets`): an outfit painted whole on a "dressing pose" figure
  is cut into one piece per bone. Each pixel goes to the nearest bone label, and each piece is extended under the pieces
  drawn over it.
- **`tools/part_sheet.py`** (`templates | cut | fit | list | check | assets | unface`): cuts painted sheets (faces,
  hairs, helms, capes, weapons) into parts. It warns "runs off the cell (it's cut there)" when art is clipped by its
  cell, which is one source of cropping.
- **`tools/weapon_alone.py`** (`template | cut | preview | assets | restand`):
  - The item is painted alone and upright on the template, with the grip at `GRIP_ROW`=640. `cut` finds each column's
    grip (`grip_of`) and keys it off the background.
  - `HOLDS` (line ~76) gives each kind an angle and scale. `held()` (line ~225) draws the item into the base's fist.
  - **Important:** when the same id appears in two columns (near and far hand), `cut()` (line ~285) draws **both into
    one held picture**, `weapons/<id>.png`.
- **`tools/import_parts.py`** (`[--check] [--vector]`) builds everything the game loads:
  - **Recipes:** reads `Story/13-art-prompts.md` (the `**Basis:**` lines, the §7 recipe table, the §7.1 gear table
    and the §7.2 party-gear table). `RECIPE_SLOTS` (line 404) = face, armor, cape, helm, weapon; **there is no offhand
    column**.
  - **Sockets:** `Base.measure()` finds them (`SOCKETS`: root, neck, crown, chest, back, hand.near, hand.far).
    `SLOT_SOCKET` (line 116) puts `weapon` on `hand.near` and `offhand` on `hand.far`.
  - **Weapon and offhand layers:** `hand_layer()` (line 1353) works out the pixels that changed between the base and
    the held picture, using `changed_pieces()` (line 1321: a colour difference over `CHANGED`=48, then opened and
    regrown). It keeps **every changed piece within `HAND_REACH`=90 px of either hand**, as one layer. Only ids
    containing "shield" or "buckler" keep skin-coloured pixels.
  - **Armor pieces:** `cut_pieces()` (line 630). `keep_near_main()` (line 1641) exists, but it is **not applied to rig
    pieces**.
  - **Scaling and cropping:** `shrink()` (line 558) resamples to `PART_SCALE`=0.5 (Mitchell, premultiplied colour)
    and trims to alpha > 0. **A part's rectangle is simply its alpha bounding box after keying.** Anything outside the
    512×768 scaled canvas is lost.
  - **Pivot:** socket × 0.5 − layer origin (`placed()` in `build()`).
  - **Packing:** `pack()` (line 1661) shelf-packs each slot into pages of 2048 px or less, with `PAD`=2 transparent
    pixels between parts.
  - **Output:** `Content/Characters/Parts/parts.json` (version 6), the atlas pages `armor-N.png`, `weapon-N.png`,
    `offhand-0.png` and so on, `clips.json` (via `clips.py`), and its sections of `Content.mgcb` and `ASSETS.md`.
    Previews go to `shots/art/parts/`.
  - **Caching:** helms and armors are cached in `tools/.cache/parts`. Weapons and offhands are re-cut every run.

### 1.2 Runtime (C#)

- **`Characters/Parts/PartCatalog.cs`:**
  - `LoadContent` cuts each part out of its atlas page by its rect into a separate image, so atlas bleed can't happen
    in game.
  - `Composed()` builds a figure from the body pieces, head, face, armor, cape, helm, `Outfit.Weapon`, `Outfit.Offhand`
    and extras.
  - `OutfitOf(member)` fills `Offhand` from party equipment. `Outfit.Of(recipe)` (`Characters/Parts/Outfit.cs`)
    always passes `Offhand: null`, so NPCs and foes built from recipes can't hold an offhand.
- **`Characters/Parts/RigComposer.cs`:**
  - `Compose()` puts each part's pivot on its socket and makes one layer per bone (plus `Cape`, `Weapon`, `Offhand`).
  - Draw order follows `DRAW`: far arm → legs → torso → skirt → head → near arm → near hand. **The near arm and near
    hand are drawn after (in front of) the head.**
  - `Weapon` is inserted just before `hand_near`. `Offhand` goes before `hand_far`, or after it for shields
    (`IsShield`).
  - `RidesOn()`: Cape rides the torso, Weapon rides `hand_near`, Offhand rides `hand_far`.
  - `SideFoot` swaps the near foot's picture for running.
- **`Characters/ImportedFigure.cs`:** scales each piece to room size and trims it to alpha > 0, with no transparent
  border.
- **`Characters/WalkerSprite.cs`:** `DrawRigged` (line ~798) solves the skeleton (`Characters/Parts/Skeleton.cs`) and
  draws pieces back to front. It moves a clip's `front` bones to just after the head. It also draws the skirt and cape
  springs, the weapon smear (`Characters/Motion/WeaponTrail.cs`, `WeaponShape.Of`), ghosts and the cast glow. The
  world is drawn with `AlphaBlend` + `PointClamp` (`Screens/ExploreScreen.CombatDraw.cs`).
- **Sheet creatures** (sea-wraith, gloomfang, thornmother, vault-construct): `Content/Creatures/<id>.png`, generated by
  `tools/generate_creatures.py`, 10 columns × 4 facings. Frame sizes are in `Characters/Creatures.cs`, and poses
  (column, offset, scale, lean, tint, alpha) in `Characters/Motion/CreatureMotion.cs`.

### 1.3 Animation clips

- **`tools/rig/clips.py`** writes `clips.json` (format `"LegendQuest rig clips 1"`). A clip has:
  - `length`, `loop`, `ease`;
  - per-key eases `in | out | back | hold`, written as `[t, v, "back"]`;
  - `bones{bone:{rot|x|y|sx|sy:[[t,v],...]}}`;
  - `events` (`step, hit, release, pass, telegraph, recover, land, fx, clap`);
  - `trail` windows, `swaps`, `layer`, `fx`, `front`, `speed`, `blend`, `partner`, `face`, `expr` and `notes`.

  Post-passes are `plant()`, `drag()` and `spring_skirt()`. `READY` (line 133) is the combat stance; it lifts the near
  arm (`arm_near_up` −30, `arm_near_low` −72).
- **`tools/rig/combat_clips.py`**: all auto-attack, skill, foe, telegraph and victory clips (about 320 clips and 55
  aliases).
- **`python tools/rig/clips.py --check`** fails on any of these:
  - a foot under the ground;
  - a knee or elbow bent backward;
  - a planted foot that slides;
  - a missing clip the fight needs;
  - a blow with no `hit` or `release`;
  - two skill or foe clips that look alike (`likeness()`, time-warped).
- **`python tools/rig/clip_strip.py <out.png> <clip>... [--fps N --gif --base B --armor A --weapon W --offhand O --per-row N]`**
  draws contact sheets of clips posed on the source pieces. Use it to look at your work.

### Problem 1: The raised near arm covers the face (layering). Severity: high. Affects every humanoid.

- In wind-ups and overhead poses, `arm_near_up` and its pauldron are drawn over the head and the face disappears.
- **Seen in:** the Voyage proof's `rig-windup.png` and the tutorial fight shot (Caleb, the player character, during
  the `auto-one-hand-1` wind-up), and in offline strips of `auto-one-hand-1` frame 1.
- **Cause:** fixed draw order (near arm after head). No clip moves the near arm behind the head while the hand is above
  the shoulder, and the wind-up keys bring the elbow across the face.

### Problem 2: Weapon and offhand pictures are fragments or the wrong thing. Severity: high.

These items are only scraps, because the import kept only "changed pixels near a hand". Where the item overlapped the
body or matched its colours, it was dropped.

- **Weapons with missing pieces:**
  - `weapon-battleaxe`: handle scraps, no head.
  - `weapon-cudgel`: used by combat foes.
  - `weapon-light-crossbow`, `weapon-pike`, `weapon-hammer-tongs`, `weapon-pilgrim-staff`, `weapon-practice-sword`.
  - `weapon-warhammer`: 79×55 px, far too small.
  - `weapon-sera-longbow`: 10 fragments; the largest is 26%. Part of the bow ended up in `armor-sera`'s `hand_near`
    piece.
- **Offhands that are glove or wrap scraps, not weapons:** `offhand-dagger`, `offhand-ember-dagger`, `offhand-handaxe`,
  `offhand-scimitar` and `offhand-shortsword` (all on `offhand-0.png`). In game, a silver squiggle floats at Caleb's
  far fist.
- **Tiny or wrong:** `weapon-crate` (13×31 px), `weapon-stew-pot` (a cloth strip, not a pot), `weapon-spindle` (9×123).

### Problem 3: Opaque backgrounds on held items (missing alpha). Severity: high.

- **White card behind the item:** `weapon-sunsteel-blade` (player gear), `weapon-mallet`, `weapon-spade`,
  `weapon-heron-cane`, `weapon-netting-needle`, `weapon-rag-doll`, `weapon-loaf`, `weapon-tankard`. The matching

- **Black shapes:**
  - `weapon-lantern` is a solid black silhouette.
  - `weapon-smoker` and `weapon-sketchbook` are mostly black.
  - `weapon-glass-lamp` has a black crescent.
  - `weapon-hexer-staff` has a dark disc around its orb that covers the Hollowed Hexer's head and body in fights.

### Problem 4: Two items baked into one picture (splitting). Severity: high for Brannock, medium overall.

- **Brannock's case, confirmed:**
  - `weapon-sheets/weapons-08.json` lists `weapon-brannock/near/blade` and `weapon-brannock/far/shield`.
    `weapon_alone.py` keeps them as `weapons-alone/weapon-brannock.png` and `weapon-brannock.far.png`, but then draws
    both into one held picture.
  - `import_parts.hand_layer()` keeps both hands' pixels as one layer on `hand.near` (`weapon-0` rect
    1008,317 401×259).
  - So the shield rides the **sword** arm and covers his face in the ready stance.
  - Story/13 §7.2 lists his off hand as `wooden-shield` with no part (`—`).
- **Other items with a `.far` half that share the problem** (26 in `weapons-alone/`): `weapon-mace-shield`,
  `weapon-unmade-spear-shield`, `weapon-kael-knives`, `weapon-kael-rapier`, `weapon-sb-blades`, `weapon-sera-longbow`,
  and the `*-kit` items (`raider-kit`, `relief-kit`, `watch-kit`, `warden-kit`, `gilded-kit`, `protectorate-kit`,
  `corwin-kit`, …).
- A combined picture also confuses the weapon smear: `WeaponShape.Of` finds the grip-to-tip axis from the picture's
  farthest pixels.

### Problem 5: Armor limb pieces carry stray islands (splitting). Severity: medium-high.

- **225 of 864 rig pieces** have detached islands (at least 4 px, at least 4 px from the main shape). When the limb
  turns, the islands swing off as "confetti".
  - **By bone:** lower legs 59, upper legs 55, upper arms 24, skirt 24, hand_near 20, torso 17, lower arms 14,
    feet 10.
  - **Worst parts:** `armor-sera` (10 pieces), `armor-kael` 9, `armor-hide` 8, `armor-traveller` 8 (Caleb's default),
    `armor-chain-mail`, `armor-chain-shirt-f`, `armor-elara`, `armor-mage-robes-f`, `armor-sb-traveller`,
    `armor-villager-woman`, `armor-watch` (7 each).
- **Examples:**
  - `armor-sera` `hand_near` contains the pale bow limb (a 2641 px island).
  - `armor-kael` `hand_near` contains a long dark blade or coat strip (3151 px).
  - `armor-hollowed-hexer` `foot_far` has a robe-hem triangle.
  - `armor-traveller` has specks off the hips, and a hard horizontal cut across the coat back under the belt pouch.
- **Cause:** `cut_pieces()` and `dress_pose.py cut` give coat, cape and weapon pixels to whichever limb label is
  nearest. Islands are never removed or reassigned.
- **Holes:** 26 rig pieces have enclosed holes of 150 px or more, mostly skirts and torsos. Most are probably painted
  gaps between coat tails; check each one.

### Problem 6: Fist-shaped holes in held items. Severity: medium.

- 35 weapon and offhand pictures have a fist-shaped transparent window where the painted hand was removed. Examples:
  `weapon-netting-needle` 10111 px, `weapon-scrolls` 3767, `weapon-loaf` 3457, and the three offhand shields.
- They rely on the hand piece sitting exactly under the hole. With a different glove, the female fit of a male item,
  or a pose change, the background shows through the item.

### Problem 7: Clipped art at frame and cell edges. Severity: low to medium.

- `Content/Creatures/gloomfang.png`: in 24 of 40 frames the eye-smoke wisp is cut flat at the frame top.
- `thornmother.png`: the crystal tip is cut in 2 frames (row 1 and row 2, column 5).
- `hair-42` (used by Brenna): spikes cut flat at the top. `hair-20` and `hair-39` may be too.

### Problem 8: Other animation and feel problems found in review

- **Shared poses:**
  - Riposte (Swordhand) is hard to read: an ally stands in front of the player in the proof.
  - The Hex (Hollowed Hexer) and Smoke Bomb (Kael) films show little of the arm.
  - Unbroken Oath's "kneel" is only a deep crouch.
  - Armour of Shadows' cloak sweep is mostly hidden behind the body.
- **Rendering:**
  - Any far-hand `hand_cast` clip with a staff tilts the staff for one frame at release.
  - Hit flash: the whole Thornmother turns pure opaque white with blocky edges for 2–3 frames.
  - The red telegraph ellipse is drawn as chunky stair-steps.
- **HUD and screen:** the hotbar and cast bar cover fighters' feet: the fight area runs under the HUD.
- **Timing:** in multi-blow combos the clip can fall behind the damage after big hit-stops (engine side). A downed
  leader is drawn standing for about 500 ms.
- **Faces:** the Hollowed foes' faces are blank. This may be intended ("empty-eyed"), so ask before changing it.

## 3. The plan, in order

Do the steps in order. Each step lists its acceptance checks.

### Step 1: Build a parts checker first

Write `tools/rig/check_parts.py` (and add it to `ASSETS.md` like the other scripts; see `check_assets.py`). It loads
`Content/Characters/Parts/parts.json` and the atlas pages, crops every part's rect, and reports per part:

- **Islands:** connected components (alpha > 8) other than the largest, of at least 4 px and at least 4 px from the
  main shape. List the sizes.
- **Enclosed holes:** transparent regions of at least 150 px that don't touch the bounding box edge.
- **Flat-cut edges:** an opaque run of at least 20 px covering at least 50% of a rect edge.
- **Opaque card:** the share of opaque pixels that are near-white (all channels > 235) or near-black (all < 50)
  touching the rect border, or making up more than 30% of the part.
- **Fragmented singles:** for weapon, offhand, helm and hair parts, the largest component is under 70% of the opaque
  pixels.
- **Bleed:** any alpha > 8 in the 2 px ring outside the rect.

It also checks creature sheets (frame sizes from `Characters/Creatures.cs`) for art touching a frame edge. Print a
summary and the worst offenders. Give it a `--json` output and an allow-list file for intended cases (ear pairs,
earrings, painted gaps in coats), so it can be used as a regression check. Run it now and save the baseline numbers.

**Accept when:** it reproduces the audit's numbers roughly (about 225 rig pieces with islands, about 32 broken
weapon/offhand pictures, 8 white cards, the lantern and smoker black shapes, gloomfang 24 frames).

### Step 2: Split two-handed items into a weapon and an offhand (Brannock first)

1. **`tools/weapon_alone.py`:** stop drawing the near and far halves into one held picture. The near column becomes
   `weapons/<id>.png` (weapon slot, `hand.near`). The far column becomes an offhand part (`offhand-<id>` or a named
   item such as `offhand-brannock-shield`) held in the far hand (`hand.far`), made from
